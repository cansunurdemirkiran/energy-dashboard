# Global Energy Transition Dashboard

An interactive data analysis project exploring how the world is shifting from fossil fuels to renewable energy sources — who leads, who lags, and what drives the transformation.

---

## Project Description

This project analyses global energy data from 1990 to 2022 across 65+ countries, examining:
- Growth of renewable energy sources (solar, wind, hydro)
- Decline of fossil fuel dependency
- Relationship between wealth, CO₂ emissions, and clean energy adoption
- Regional differences across continents

The project includes three Jupyter notebooks for data cleaning, exploratory analysis, and storytelling, plus a fully interactive Plotly Dash dashboard with six chart types.

## Key Findings

- **Global renewable share grew from ~20% (1990) to ~35% (2022)** — real progress, but far below the pace needed for net-zero by 2050.
- **Solar and wind were negligible before 2005**; post-2010 growth is exponential, driven by falling costs and supportive policy.
- **No simple wealth–renewables relationship:** Gulf states are among the richest nations but have near-zero renewables; Brazil and Norway combine high income with dominant clean energy.
- **Europe leads on pace of transition** — Denmark, Germany, and the UK have roughly doubled their renewables share since 2000.
- **Africa and South America lead on current share** thanks to large hydro endowments, but solar and wind offer pathways for continued growth.
